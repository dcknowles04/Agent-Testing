Dear Appeals Reviewer:

We request reversal of the medical necessity denial for the lumbar MRI performed on
{{DOS|long}}. The denial states that no red-flag findings were identified. The enclosed
records show that Mr. Toller developed a new and progressing motor deficit before the MRI
was ordered, which meets Clinical Guideline CG-RAD-12, Indication B1, "Progressive or
severe neurological deficit (e.g., new or worsening motor weakness)." Under Indication B1,
no conservative-therapy period is required.

Serial examinations document the progression, as the guideline requests:
- {{VISIT1|long}} (Enclosure 1): strength 5/5 throughout, including the left extensor
  hallucis longus and ankle dorsiflexion; left L5 sensory change only.
- {{VISIT2|long}} (Enclosure 2): left EHL 4/5 and left ankle dorsiflexion 4+/5, with the
  patient reporting that his "foot slaps when tired." Dr. Okafor documented a "NEW motor
  deficit... progressing since the initial exam despite conservative care."

His treating physical therapist independently recorded new difficulty with heel walking
on the left on {{PT_LAST|long}} and referred him back to the physician (Enclosure 3).
This decline occurred despite oral steroids, scheduled naproxen, activity modification and
six physical therapy visits.

The MRI confirmed the clinical concern: a 9 mm left paracentral L4-5 disc extrusion
compressing the traversing left L5 nerve root, concordant with his deficit (Enclosure 4).
The study was ordered to determine whether epidural steroid injection or surgical referral
was needed, which is also consistent with Indication C (pre-procedural planning).

A new motor deficit from nerve root compression is exactly the situation in which
CG-RAD-12 calls for prompt imaging rather than waiting out a six-week course of therapy.
We respectfully request that the claim be reprocessed for payment.
